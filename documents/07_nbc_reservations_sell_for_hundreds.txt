Reservations at Top New York City Restaurants Are Selling for Hundreds of Dollars
Source: NBC News (https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/reservations-top-new-york-city-restaurants-are-selling-hundreds-dollar-rcna151702)

At the hardest-to-book restaurants in New York City, a reservation itself has become a product with a price. On secondary marketplaces, diners pay a premium — frequently hundreds of dollars — just to claim a table, before spending a cent on the actual meal. The phenomenon has turned scarce reservations into a tradable asset.

The mechanics behind it: a class of sellers uses early access, multiple accounts, and in some cases automated bots to grab prime reservations the instant they are released. Those reservations are then listed for resale, and buyers bid against each other. Restaurants that consistently command high resale prices include marquee names where demand vastly outstrips the number of seats.

The downside for restaurants and ordinary diners: when reservations are hoarded for resale, genuine guests are shut out, and unsold resale inventory can become last-minute cancellations or no-shows that leave tables empty. Restaurateurs argue this damages both their revenue and their relationship with regular customers, since the people who most want to dine there can't get in without paying a middleman.

The reporting highlights the tension that prompted regulatory action: reservations are free to make but increasingly impossible to get at face value, which created the opening for a paid resale economy — and the backlash that followed, including legislative efforts to ban the practice in New York.
