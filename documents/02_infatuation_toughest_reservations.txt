The Toughest Reservations In NYC Right Now (And How To Get Them)
Source: The Infatuation — New York (https://www.theinfatuation.com/new-york/guides/toughest-restaurant-reservations-nyc)

This guide tracks the hardest tables in New York and the specific tactics that actually work for each one — when reservations are released, which platform they live on, and whether walk-ins are realistic.

Dean's
How To Get In: Dean's releases reservations two weeks in advance at 9am. Keep in mind, all eight bar seats and most of the tables are saved for walk-ins. On a recent Wednesday at 6pm, we were quoted a one-hour wait for a table for two.

Tascha
How To Get In: Tascha releases Thursday (and sometimes Friday) reservations one week in advance, and you can only book via text or Instagram DM. In our experience, the DM route is more effective. It's a long shot, but you can also try walking in. It worked for us once.

Marcel
How To Get In: Reservations for Marcel are released 28 days in advance. All of the bar seats are saved for walk-ins, and they serve the full menu at the bar.

Odo East Village
How To Get In: Odo East Village only has 14 seats, all at a counter, and reservations for those seats are released 30 days out at midnight. Walk-ins are technically accepted, but don't count on getting in that way. We came at 6pm on a Friday as a party of two and were told there was no availability. (It may be easier if you're a party of one.)

A Midtown spot booked through DoorDash
How To Get In: Reservations are released on DoorDash seven days in advance. The restaurant does accept limited walk-ins, and if that's the route you're going, line up outside by 4pm at the latest. Around 4:25pm, a host will start taking names, and they'll send you a text if a table or bar seat becomes available.

Ambassadors Clubhouse
How To Get In: For now, Ambassadors Clubhouse releases reservations two weeks in advance. Eventually, it'll be four weeks. Walk-ins are accepted, but there don't seem to be many slots. According to a host, if you want to eat here without a reservation, your odds improve the later it gets.

Bistrot Ha
How To Get In: Bistrot Ha releases reservations six days in advance at midnight. The place does take walk-ins, and the line outside tends to start forming around 4:45pm. On a recent Thursday at 9:30pm, the wait for bar seats was around four hours. So be sure to come early.

Ramen By Ra
How To Get In: Ramen By Ra doesn't take walk-ins. Reservations are required, and they're released in two-week blocks on the 1st and 15th of the month at 9am. (To eat on the 20th, you'll have to reserve on the 1st.) In our experience, it's not hard to snag a seat as long as you're online at exactly 9am. You can also stop by for takeaway broth or a breakfast bao in the morning.

Sushi Sho
How To Get In: Sushi Sho allows you to book two months in advance, with new reservations released on the first of the month at 11am. In other words, if you want a seat in January, stay glued to the reservations page on December 1.

Semma
How To Get In: Reservations are released online two weeks in advance. There aren't any tables saved for walk-ins, but there are 12 seats at the bar, so you can always try your luck there.

The Eighty Six
How To Get In: Reservations for The Eighty Six are released two weeks in advance. According to the restaurant, two to four walk-ins are accepted at the bar every night, but we've seen them seat as many as six at opening. If you want to eat here, arrive no later than 4:30pm.

A see-and-be-seen spot
How To Get In: Reservations are released online two weeks in advance, but if you can't snag one, they do take walk-ins at the bar. When we came at 4:50pm on a Wednesday, there were around eight walk-ins ahead of us in line, and we all got seated at 5pm when the restaurant opened. If it's a weekend, get here by 4:30pm.

Bong
How To Get In: Bong releases reservations 20 days in advance at midnight. If you're in the area, you can try walking in — they have extra outdoor tables when it's warm — but officially they're reservation-only.

I Cavallini
How To Get In: I Cavallini releases reservations two weeks in advance at 8am. All of the bar and counter seats are saved for walk-ins, and you can also get a walk-in table during the first seating. The trick is to line up outside no later than 4:30pm. When the place opens at 5pm, you should be seated right away.

Una Pizza Napoletana
How To Get In: Una Pizza Napoletana is open Thursday through Saturday, and they release reservations two weeks in advance at 9am. Walk-ins are only accepted for drinks and dessert.

General tactics from this guide: be online at the exact release time down to the minute; learn which platform each restaurant uses (Resy, Tock, DoorDash, SevenRooms, OpenTable, or direct text/DM); and treat bar seats and early walk-in lines as a parallel path when reservations are gone.
