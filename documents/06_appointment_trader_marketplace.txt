Appointment Trader: The Secondary Market for Restaurant Reservations
Source: Appointment Trader (https://appointmenttrader.com/)

Appointment Trader is a marketplace where people buy and sell coveted restaurant reservations. Instead of competing for a table when it drops, a buyer can pay to claim a reservation that someone else booked. The cost of the reservation is on top of what you'll spend on food and drinks, and at New York's most in-demand restaurants that premium can easily run into the hundreds of dollars.

How it works: sellers — the platform has reported around 15,000 of them — book or hold the best reservations and then list them on Appointment Trader. Buyers place bids, and the higher the demand for a given restaurant, date, and time, the higher the final price. Reservations at buzzy spots like 4 Charles Prime Rib, Don Angie, and Carbone have been among the most sought-after listings.

Origin and scale: the platform was created by Jonas Frey, who has said the idea came after a frustrating day at the DMV, when he wondered why he couldn't simply pay to get an appointment. He launched Appointment Trader in 2021. The company has reported selling several million dollars' worth of reservations in a single year, with buyers spending tens of thousands of dollars a day during peak periods.

Why it matters for getting hard reservations: the resale market is one reason prime tables disappear instantly — some are claimed specifically to be resold. It also raises cost and legal questions. New York moved to ban unauthorized reservation resale, which directly affects platforms like this one. Anyone considering buying a reservation should weigh the markup, the risk that a restaurant or booking platform cancels a resold reservation, and the changing legal status of the practice.
